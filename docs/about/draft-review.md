## Mathematical qualifications made explicit

1. **Canonical bornology:** on a disconnected coarse space, take the bornology generated by coarsely bounded sets.
   The condition $B\times B\in\mathcal C$ alone need not be closed under finite unions.
2. **Partition classes:** the Ludewig–Thiang construction is in coarse cohomology.
   The article gives its cocycle and its pairing with coarse homology.
3. **Ray factorization:** factoring the identity through $X\otimes\mathbb N$ characterizes generalized flasqueness.
   The article also states the usual iterated-map definition and the action condition relating them.
4. **Point objects:** locality gives a pointwise colimit decomposition.
   Local finiteness of the nontrivial point objects follows in the finite-dimensional Hilbert and matrix cases; it is not automatic for arbitrary coefficients.
5. **Derivations:** the proof concerns the algebraic local algebra.
   It uses matrix-unit innerness, not an unjustified extension of a bimodule derivation by tensoring with the identity.
   No Hamiltonian dynamics is inferred.
6. **Spectral theorem:** a nondegenerate $C_0(X)$-action extends to bounded Borel functions.
   All bounded functions are available in the discrete model.
7. **Ample modules:** their comparison is a controlled unitary equivalence, not necessarily an intertwining of the $C_0(X)$-representations.
8. **Index representatives:** the trace condition is local.
   An odd polynomial representative need not be unitary or have a finite-propagation inverse.
9. **Roe algebra and coarse K-theory:** the author has replaced these drafts with stubs.
   Their earlier definitions and arguments are no longer supplied in the current collection.
10. **QCA:** the spectrum construction uses the stable coarse quotient.
    The plane-vanishing discussion compares it with the one-dimensional Brauer group; it does not assert circuit triviality on a fixed spin system.
    The article links directly to the public papers for the results used.
