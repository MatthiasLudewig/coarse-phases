# Coarse Geometry and Topological Phases

This is a mathematical wiki about coarse geometry and its applications in condensed matter physics.
Coarse geometry studies the large-scale structure of space and is designed to axiomatize phenomena of *uniform control*, which are omnipresent in condensed matter physics.
Although less well known, the concept of a coarse space is very simple and as fundamental as that of a topological or metric space.

The suitability of the coarse framework for homotopy theory is a reason to prefer coarse geometry over metric geometry, the geometry of metric spaces, even though the latter is more familiar to most people.
Spreading familiarity with coarse spaces is therefore one of the main goals of this wiki.

A particular task in condensed matter is the classification of topological phases.
The {ref}`Freed–Hopkins framework <bib-freed-hopkins>` approaches this classification for invertible phases using invertible topological field theories; see their [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06527).
However, the connection between this framework and real-time descriptions using lattice systems remains unclear.
We hope that coarse homotopy theory and its relation to ordinary homotopy theory will help close this gap.
