(bibliography)=
# Bibliography

These are the sources used for the first article drafts. References to the
supplied unpublished manuscripts identify their status; they are not claims
of publication or independent verification. The private source collection is
not part of the repository.

(bib-bunke-engel)=
## Bunke–Engel: bornological coarse spaces

Ulrich Bunke and Alexander Engel, *Homotopy theory with bornological coarse
spaces*, Lecture Notes in Mathematics **2269**, Springer, 2020.
[Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03657).
The section and theorem numbers in these drafts refer to the supplied preprint
where explicitly stated.

(bib-callias)=
## Bunke–Ludewig: coronas and Callias operators

Ulrich Bunke and Matthias Ludewig, *Coronas and Callias type operators in coarse
geometry*. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01646).
The supplied revised LaTeX manuscript was used for the definitions of big
families, the two-sided cone, and big-family coronas.

(bib-large-scale-trace)=
## Ludewig–Thiang: large-scale trace quantization

Matthias Ludewig and Guo Chuan Thiang, *Large-scale quantization of trace I:
Finite propagation operators*. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10957).
The supplied 2026 revision was used for coarse transversality, the partition
cocycle, the measure model of coarse homology, and the locally trace-class
operator algebra.

(bib-roe-cohomology)=
## Roe: coarse cohomology and index theory

John Roe, *Coarse cohomology and index theory on complete Riemannian manifolds*,
Memoirs of the American Mathematical Society **104**, no. 497, 1993.
The supplied book was consulted, in particular §§4.1–4.3 and §5.1.

(bib-higson-roe)=
## Higson–Roe: analytic K-homology

Nigel Higson and John Roe, *Analytic K-homology*, Oxford Mathematical
Monographs, Oxford University Press, 2000.
For the ample-module comparison, the attribution to §6.3 is also recorded in
the supplied Ewert–Meyer paper; the proof was checked there.

(bib-ewert-meyer)=
## Ewert–Meyer: coarse geometry and topological phases

Eske Ellen Ewert and Ralf Meyer, *Coarse geometry and topological phases*.
The supplied paper was consulted, particularly §2 and Theorem 2.1, which
provides the bounded-partition proof of unitary equivalence of ample modules.

(bib-coarse-k-categories)=
## Bunke–Engel: categorical coarse K-homology

Ulrich Bunke and Alexander Engel, *Topological equivariant coarse K-homology*.
[Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13271).
The locally finite Roe-category model used in the wiki is already given in
Chapter 8 of their bornological coarse spaces book.

(bib-gnvw)=
## Gross–Nesme–Vogts–Werner: the one-dimensional index

David Gross, Vincent Nesme, Holger Vogts, and Reinhard F. Werner,
*Index theory of one dimensional quantum walks and cellular automata*,
Communications in Mathematical Physics **310** (2012), 419–454.
[Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.3675).
The support-algebra definition and its classification properties are in §7.

(bib-haah)=
## Haah: invertible subalgebras

Jeongwan Haah, *Invertible subalgebras*, Communications in Mathematical Physics
**403** (2023), 661–698.
[Version 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02086v4).
Theorem B.5 proves that the one-dimensional Brauer group is zero. The paper's
QCA quotient uses blending equivalence and shifts; comparisons with other
quotients must state their stabilization conventions.

(bib-higson-kato)=
## Higson–Kato: locally generated derivations

Nigel Higson and Tsuyoshi Kato, *Homology of the Lie Algebra of Locally Generated
Derivations of a Discrete and Proper Metric Space*.
[Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08455).
The supplied version 1 was used, particularly §§2 and 7.

(bib-mitchener)=
## Mitchener: coarse homology theories

Paul D. Mitchener, *Coarse homology theories*, Algebraic & Geometric Topology
**1** (2001), 271–297.
[Original article](https://doi.org/10.2140/agt.2001.1.271).
This is a reference for the group-valued axiomatic viewpoint; the main
formulation in the wiki follows the supplied Bunke–Engel and QCA texts.

(bib-freed-hopkins)=
## Freed–Hopkins: invertible topological phases

Daniel S. Freed and Michael J. Hopkins, *Reflection positivity and invertible
topological phases*. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06527).
The stub uses only the stated scope of their field-theoretic classification.

## Author-supplied manuscripts and notes

The following sources are unpublished material supplied for drafting. They
remain outside the uploaded repository. Titles identify the documents without
exposing the source files.

(bib-controlled-notes)=
### Controlled objects

Matthias Ludewig, *Controlled objects*, supplied manuscript. Used for controlled
objects, local tensor extensions, the point-object proposition, and Azumaya
objects. The wiki makes the coefficient-category finiteness assumptions explicit.

(bib-systems-notes)=
### Local nets and gapped systems

Matthias Ludewig, *Local nets and gapped systems*, supplied `Systems` manuscript.
Used for geometric modules, local matrix nets, conditional expectations,
exact-support decompositions, and controlled derivations. The finite-dimensional
innerness step in the derivation proof is expanded using matrix units.

(bib-qca-notes)=
### Quantum cellular automata are a coarse homology theory

Matthias Ludewig, *Quantum cellular automata are a coarse homology theory*,
supplied `QCAcoarse` manuscript. Used for the stable coarse QCA group,
Azumaya-net construction, and the proposed spectrum-valued homology theory.
Its manuscript status is retained in the article.

(bib-sparse-notes)=
### Sparse sets

Matthias Ludewig, *Sparse sets*, supplied notes. Used for the definition through
coarsely dense complements of thickenings and the big family of sparse subsets.

(bib-generated-families)=
### Singly generated big families

Matthias Ludewig, *Singly generated big families*, supplied notes. Used for the
caution that intersecting generated big families is different from generating
a family from the intersection of two subsets.

(bib-finite-propagation-notes)=
### Odd generators of finite propagation

Matthias Ludewig, *Odd generators of finite propagation*, supplied note.
Used for the polynomial replacement of an exponential index representative.
The wiki states local trace class and distinguishes invertibility in the
completion from a finite-propagation inverse.
