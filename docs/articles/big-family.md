# Big family

```{warning}
Agent-generated, not reviewed yet
```

A single subset of a coarse space does not encode a support condition invariant
under thickening. The appropriate replacement is the collection of all subsets
allowed by that support condition.

## Definition

Let $X$ be a {prf:ref}`bornological coarse space <def-bornological-coarse-space>`.

```{prf:definition} Big family
:nonumber:
:label: def-big-family

A big family on $X$ is a nonempty collection $\mathcal Y\subseteq\mathcal P(X)$
closed under subsets, finite unions, and coarse thickenings:

$$
Y\in\mathcal Y,\ E\in\mathcal C_X\quad\Longrightarrow\quad E[Y]\in\mathcal Y.
$$

```

The family need not cover $X$. In particular, $\{\varnothing\}$ is a big
family. The bornology $\mathcal B_X$ and $\mathcal P(X)$ are also big families.

We use complete, self-indexing big families. An increasing family $(Y_i)_{i\in I}$
indexed by a filtered poset, with each thickening of $Y_i$ contained in some
$Y_j$, gives the same support data after passing to
$\{A\subseteq X:A\subseteq Y_i\text{ for some }i\}$.

## Elementwise intersection and union

For big families $\mathcal Y,\mathcal Z$, put

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Y\Cap\mathcal Z
 &=\{Y\cap Z:Y\in\mathcal Y,\ Z\in\mathcal Z\},\\
\mathcal Y\Cup\mathcal Z
 &=\{Y\cup Z:Y\in\mathcal Y,\ Z\in\mathcal Z\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Both are big families. For intersection this follows from
$E[Y\cap Z]\subseteq E[Y]\cap E[Z]$; for union it follows from
$E[Y\cup Z]=E[Y]\cup E[Z]$. Closure under subsets and finite unions follows
by distributing intersections and unions and using downward closure.

Because the families are downward closed, $\mathcal Y\Cap\mathcal Z$ is also
the ordinary set-theoretic intersection $\mathcal Y\cap\mathcal Z$.
The operation $\Cup$, however, is generally larger than ordinary union: it is
the smallest big family containing both families. These operations give a
distributive lattice of support conditions.

## Families generated by subsets

```{prf:definition} Generated big family
:nonumber:
:label: def-generated-big-family

For $A\subseteq X$, write

$$
\{A\}=\{Y\subseteq X:\exists E\in\mathcal C_X,\ Y\subseteq E[A]\}.
$$

This is the smallest big family containing $A$.
```

Here braces denote a big family, not the singleton collection consisting of
$A$. For a metric space, its members are the subsets of finite-radius
neighbourhoods of $A$. For example, the family generated by a line in
$\mathbb R^2$ consists of subsets of strips of finite width around that line.

One has $\{A\}\Cup\{B\}=\{A\cup B\}$. In contrast,
$\{A\cap B\}\subseteq\{A\}\Cap\{B\}$ can be strict. For the two disjoint
parallel lines $\mathbb R\times\{0\}$ and $\mathbb R\times\{1\}$, the
right-hand side is the family of strips, whereas the left-hand side is
$\{\varnothing\}$.

The coarse boundary of a subset $A$ is the big family

$$
\partial A:=\{A\}\Cap\{X\setminus A\}.
$$

It records the controlled overlap of the two sides. It is used in
{ref}`coarse transversality <article-coarse-transversality>` and coarse excision.

For a homology theory $E$, evaluation on a big family means
$E(\mathcal Y)=\operatorname*{colim}_{Y\in\mathcal Y}E(Y)$, using the induced
coarse structures on the members.

## Sparse subsets

Sparsity is a condition on the complements of all thickenings, not merely a
condition that points become far apart.

```{prf:definition} Sparse subset
:nonumber:
:label: def-sparse-subset

A subset $S\subseteq X$ is sparse if $X\setminus E[S]$ is coarsely dense for
every entourage $E$. Here $A$ is coarsely dense if $F[A]=X$ for some entourage
$F$.
```

For a metric space this says: for each $r$ there is an $R$ such that every
point of $X$ lies within distance $R$ of $X\setminus E_r[S]$. Thus every fixed
thickening of $S$ has bounded width. A line in $\mathbb R^2$ is sparse; the
integer lattice in $\mathbb R$ is not, since a fixed thickening fills the line.

For metric coarse structures the sparse subsets form a big family. Closure
under subsets and thickening is immediate. Here is a direct verification of
closure under unions. Given sparse $S,T$ and $r$, choose $a$ so that every point
is within $a$ of $X\setminus E_r[S]$. Then choose $b$ so that every point is
within $b$ of $X\setminus E_{r+a}[T]$. Starting at $x$, first choose such a
point $y$ outside $E_{r+a}[T]$, and then choose $z$ within $a$ of $y$ outside
$E_r[S]$. The triangle inequality shows that $z\notin E_r[T]$. Thus the
complement of $E_r[S\cup T]$ is $(a+b)$-dense. Radii may be enlarged slightly
if the infimum defining distance is not attained.
