# Kramers–Wannier channel

```{warning}
Agent-generated, not reviewed yet
```

The Kramers–Wannier channel exchanges single-site transverse-spin observables with nearest-neighbor Ising interactions and annihilates observables odd under spin flip.
It is an example of a {prf:ref}`non-invertible quantum cellular automaton <def-non-invertible-qca>` whose control holds on intervals, while a pair of distant endpoints can be mapped to the string joining them.

## The qubit chain and its even algebra

Equip $\Z$ with its usual metric coarse structure and the bornology of finite subsets.
Consider the {prf:ref}`spin system <def-quantum-spin-system>`
$$
\cA_B=\bigotimes_{j\in B}\Mat_2(\C),\qquad
\cA_\Z=\overline{\bigcup_{B\subseteq\Z,\ B\text{ finite}}\cA_B}.
$$
Write $X_j$ and $Z_j$ for the Pauli matrices
$$
X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}
$$
at site $j$, with identity operators at all other sites.
Thus $X_jZ_j=-Z_jX_j$, and operators at distinct sites commute.
Let $\tau$ be the product of the normalized matrix traces.

The *spin-flip automorphism* $\eta$ is determined by
$$
\eta(X_j)=X_j,\qquad \eta(Z_j)=-Z_j.
$$
On $\cA_B$ it is conjugation by $\prod_{j\in B}X_j$; this defines $\eta$ without requiring an infinite product to be an element of $\cA_\Z$.
Write $\cA^+=\{a\in\cA_\Z\mid\eta(a)=a\}$ for the *even algebra* and $\cA^-=\{a\in\cA_\Z\mid\eta(a)=-a\}$ for the *odd subspace*.
The odd subspace is not a subalgebra.
The projection onto the even algebra is the conditional expectation
$$
P_+=\frac12(\id+\eta):\cA_\Z\longrightarrow\cA^+.
$$
As an average of automorphisms, $P_+$ is unital and completely positive, and it preserves $\tau$.
It preserves every finite support, fixes even observables, and kills odd observables.
It also satisfies $\tau(P_+(a)b)=\tau(aP_+(b))$.

## Construction of the channel

The even algebra is generated by $p_j=X_j$ and $q_j=Z_jZ_{j+1}$.
Indeed, an even Pauli word has an even number of $Z$ factors after writing each on-site factor using $X_j$ and $Z_j$, and a pair $Z_mZ_n$ is a product of adjacent bonds $q_m\cdots q_{n-1}$.
There is a $*$-automorphism $\alpha:\cA^+\to\cA^+$ determined by
$$
\alpha(p_j)=q_{j-1},\qquad \alpha(q_j)=p_j.
$$
To see this, the generators are self-adjoint unitaries, the $p_j$ commute with one another, the $q_j$ commute with one another, and $p_j$ anticommutes with $q_k$ exactly when $j=k$ or $j=k+1$.
The displayed assignment preserves these relations and has the inverse
$$
\alpha^{-1}(p_j)=q_j,\qquad \alpha^{-1}(q_j)=p_{j+1}.
$$
Finite ordered products of distinct $p_j$ and distinct $q_j$ give the even Pauli basis, so these assignments define inverse $*$-homomorphisms on the local even algebra.
On each finite-dimensional local even algebra they are isometric, hence extend to inverse $*$-automorphisms of the completion.
They permute nonidentity Pauli words up to phase and therefore preserve $\tau$.

```{prf:definition} Kramers–Wannier channel
:nonumber:
:label: def-kramers-wannier-channel

The *Kramers–Wannier channel* is
$$
\cD:=\alpha\circ P_+:\cA_\Z\longrightarrow\cA^+\subseteq\cA_\Z.
$$
```

The channel is UCP and trace-preserving, acts multiplicatively on $\cA^+$, and vanishes on $\cA^-$.
In particular,
$$
\cD(X_j)=Z_{j-1}Z_j,\qquad
\cD(Z_jZ_{j+1})=X_j,\qquad
\cD(Z_j)=0.
$$
It is not injective, and it is not multiplicative on the whole algebra: $\cD(Z_j^2)=1$ whereas $\cD(Z_j)^2=0$.

## Interval control and the trace adjoint

For a finite interval $I=[m,n]\cap\Z$, its even algebra is generated by $p_j$ for $m\leq j\leq n$ and $q_j$ for $m\leq j<n$.
The formulas for $\alpha$ therefore give
$$
\cD(\cA_I)\subseteq\cA_{[m-1,n]\cap\Z}\subseteq\cA_{I_{E_1}},
\qquad E_1=\{(j,k)\mid |j-k|\leq1\}.
$$
Here $I_{E_1}$ is the {ref}`entourage fattening <def-fattening>` of the interval.

The trace adjoint is
$$
\cD^\star=\alpha^{-1}\circ P_+.
$$
Indeed, the trace-adjoint property of $P_+$ and trace preservation by $\alpha$ give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau(\cD(a)b)
&=\tau\bigl(\alpha(P_+(a))P_+(b)\bigr)\\
&=\tau\bigl(P_+(a)\alpha^{-1}(P_+(b))\bigr)\\
&=\tau\bigl(a\alpha^{-1}(P_+(b))\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
This adjoint is again UCP, kills odd observables, and satisfies
$$
\cD^\star(X_j)=Z_jZ_{j+1},\qquad
\cD^\star(Z_jZ_{j+1})=X_{j+1}.
$$
The same generator calculation gives
$$
\cD^\star(\cA_I)\subseteq\cA_{[m,n+1]\cap\Z}\subseteq\cA_{I_{E_1}}.
$$
Thus both directions satisfy the required uniform interval control.

## Fusion and purity

Since $P_+$ is the identity on $\cA^+$, direct composition gives
$$
\cD^\star\cD=\cD\cD^\star=P_+=\frac12(\id+\eta).
$$
If $T$ denotes the right translation $T(X_j)=X_{j+1}$ and $T(Z_j)=Z_{j+1}$, the generator formulas give $\alpha^2=T^{-1}|_{\cA^+}$, hence
$$
\cD^2=T^{-1}P_+=\frac12T^{-1}(\id+\eta).
$$
These identities show explicitly why the trace adjoint is not an inverse on the whole algebra.

The remaining property needed for a non-invertible QCA is CP-purity.
The channel $\cD:\cA_\Z\to\cA_\Z$ is CP-pure, as proved in [Seifnashri–Shao, §3.3, “Kramers–Wannier transformation”](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.22928v1#S3.SS3) ({ref}`bibliographic details <bib-seifnashri-shao-non-invertible-qca>`).
That proof is omitted here; the construction, support estimates, and trace-adjoint computation above verify the other axioms.
Consequently $\cD$ is a non-invertible QCA for the family of finite intervals.

Although $P_+$ is an average of two automorphisms, this does not express $\cD$ as such an average: $\alpha$ is defined only on $\cA^+$, so $\alpha\circ\id$ and $\alpha\circ\eta$ are not maps on all of $\cA_\Z$.
The relation $\cD^2=T^{-1}P_+$ also illustrates that composition of pure channels need not be pure.

## Disconnected supports and the interval-hull net

For $m<n$, the telescoping identity $Z_mZ_n=\prod_{j=m}^{n-1}(Z_jZ_{j+1})$ lies in the even algebra.
Multiplicativity there gives
$$
\cD(Z_mZ_n)=\prod_{j=m}^{n-1}X_j.
$$
The input is supported in the two-point set $B=\{m,n\}$, while the output is supported at every site from $m$ to $n-1$.
Given any fixed $r$, choose $n-m$ large enough that this interval contains a site outside $B_{E_r}$.
At such a site $k$, the output anticommutes with $Z_k$, whereas every element of $\cA_{B_{E_r}}$ commutes with $Z_k$.
Therefore the output is not in $\cA_{B_{E_r}}$.
Every entourage of the metric coarse structure is contained in some $E_r$, so $\cD$ is not controlled on all bounded subsets of the original spin-system net.

Using the {ref}`interval-hull net <non-invertible-qca-non-local-nets>` instead gives $\widetilde{\cA}_B=\cA_{h(B)}$ and
$$
\cD(\widetilde{\cA}_B)\subseteq\widetilde{\cA}_{B_{E_1}},\qquad
\cD^\star(\widetilde{\cA}_B)\subseteq\widetilde{\cA}_{B_{E_1}}
$$
for every bounded $B$.
This expresses the same channel using control on arbitrary bounded sets of a non-local net.
