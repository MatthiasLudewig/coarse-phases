# Non-invertible quantum cellular automaton

```{warning}
Agent-generated, not reviewed yet
```

A non-invertible quantum cellular automaton acts on observables by a quantum channel, with control conditions on both the channel and its trace adjoint.
Its purity condition excludes decompositions into distinct completely positive operations.
The {ref}`Kramers–Wannier channel <article-kramers-wannier-channel>` motivates testing control on specified regions: on a chain, these are intervals, and an observable supported at two endpoints may become a string between them.

## Channels and test regions

Let $(X, \cB, \cC)$ be a {prf:ref}`bornological coarse space <def-bornological-coarse-space>` and let $\cA$ be a {prf:ref}`spin system <def-quantum-spin-system>` on $X$.
Write $\tau$ for the canonical faithful tracial state on $\cA_X$, obtained from the normalized traces on the bounded-region matrix algebras.

```{prf:definition} Quantum channel
:nonumber:
:label: def-quantum-channel

A linear map $\varphi:\cA_X\to\cA_X$ is *completely positive* if its amplification
$$
\varphi^{(n)}:\Mat_n(\cA_X)\longrightarrow\Mat_n(\cA_X),\qquad
[a_{ij}]\longmapsto[\varphi(a_{ij})],
$$
is positive for every $n\geq1$.
A *quantum channel* is a unital completely positive map, abbreviated UCP.
```

Channels here act on observables, in the Heisenberg picture.
They are contractions, so a compatible UCP action on local algebras extends uniquely to the quasi-local algebra.

Fix a family $\cR\subseteq\cB$ of *test regions*, cofinal under inclusion: every bounded subset is contained in some member of $\cR$.
The choice of $\cR$ is additional data.
For $X=\Z$ with its usual metric coarse structure and the bornology of finite subsets, take $\cR$ to consist of finite intervals.
The bornology still contains all finite subsets, including disconnected ones.
For an entourage $E$, use the wiki's {ref}`fattening notation <def-fattening>` $R_E$.

## Definition

```{prf:definition} Non-invertible quantum cellular automaton
:nonumber:
:label: def-non-invertible-qca

A *non-invertible quantum cellular automaton relative to $\cR$* is a quantum channel $\varphi:\cA_X\to\cA_X$ satisfying the following conditions.

1. **Control on test regions:** there exists $E\in\cC$ such that
   $$
   \varphi(\cA_R)\subseteq\cA_{R_E}
   \qquad\text{for every }R\in\cR.
   $$
2. **Dualizability:** there exists a quantum channel $\varphi^\star:\cA_X\to\cA_X$ such that
   $$
   \tau\bigl(\varphi(a)b\bigr)=\tau\bigl(a\varphi^\star(b)\bigr)
   \qquad(a,b\in\cA_X),
   $$
   and an entourage $F\in\cC$ such that
   $$
   \varphi^\star(\cA_R)\subseteq\cA_{R_F}
   \qquad\text{for every }R\in\cR.
   $$
3. **CP-purity:** every decomposition $\varphi=\psi_1+\psi_2$ into completely positive maps $\psi_i:\cA_X\to\cA_X$ has the form $\psi_i=t_i\varphi$, where $t_i\geq0$ and $t_1+t_2=1$.
```

The entourages are uniform in the test region and the observable.
They may be replaced by one common symmetric entourage containing the diagonal.
Purity is imposed in the entire cone of completely positive maps into $\cA_X$; the summands need not be unital, controlled, or dualizable.
It is a property of the global channel, rather than of each finite-region restriction, and does not mean that the channel preserves pure states.

The map $\varphi^\star$ is the *trace adjoint* of $\varphi$.
Faithfulness of $\tau$ makes it unique: if two candidates differ by $c$ at $b$, the adjoint identity gives $\tau(ac)=0$ for every $a$, and taking $a=c^*$ gives $c=0$.
Setting $b=1$ in the identity shows that $\tau\circ\varphi=\tau$, and setting $a=1$ gives $\tau\circ\varphi^\star=\tau$.
The definition requires the trace adjoint to take values in the quasi-local algebra itself.

## Relation to ordinary QCA

The terminology allows invertible examples.
For an ordinary {prf:ref}`QCA <def-qca>` $\alpha$, its trace adjoint is $\alpha^{-1}$, since automorphisms preserve the unique normalized trace and
$$
\tau\bigl(\alpha(a)b\bigr)=\tau\bigl(a\alpha^{-1}(b)\bigr).
$$
Both maps are controlled on every bounded set, hence on the test regions.
Automorphisms of the spin-system algebra are CP-pure; the argument using the scalar center and the multiplicative domain is given in [Seifnashri–Shao, §3.3, “Invertible symmetries”](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.22928v1#S3.SS3) ({ref}`bibliographic details <bib-seifnashri-shao-non-invertible-qca>`).
That purity argument is not reproduced here.

For a spin chain, a homomorphism's control on single-site algebras implies control on every finite subset, because the latter algebra is generated by products of single-site observables.
This implication uses multiplicativity and therefore does not apply to general channels.
The {ref}`endpoint-to-string calculation <kw-channel-disconnected-supports>` gives an explicit failure.

## Remark: arbitrary bounded sets for non-local nets

One may instead work with general nets of unital $C^*$-algebras over $X$, without requiring the {prf:ref}`locality axioms <def-local-controlled-object>` of a spin system.
Given a compatible faithful tracial state on the global algebra, one may then require control on **every bounded set**, taking $\cR=\cB$ in the definition above.
With UCP maps allowed as morphisms, the two support conditions are precisely the support conditions for {prf:ref}`controlled morphisms <def-controlled-morphism>`.
A general net need not have a canonical trace, so the trace must be included in the data.

The chain case admits a concrete reformulation of this kind.
For a nonempty finite set $B\subseteq\Z$, let $h(B)=[\min B,\max B]\cap\Z$ be its *interval hull*, set $h(\varnothing)=\varnothing$, and define
$$
\widetilde{\cA}_B:=\cA_{h(B)}.
$$
This is a {prf:ref}`controlled object <def-controlled-object>` with the same global quasi-local algebra: every finite set is contained in its hull, and every interval occurs as a hull.
For the metric entourage $E_r=\{(x,y)\mid |x-y|\leq r\}$, where $r\in\N\cup\{0\}$, one has
$$
h(B_{E_r})=h(B)_{E_r}.
$$
Consequently, interval control of $\varphi$ on $\cA$ is equivalent to
$$
\varphi(\widetilde{\cA}_B)\subseteq\widetilde{\cA}_{B_{E_r}}
\qquad\text{for every finite }B\subseteq\Z,
$$
and the same equivalence holds for $\varphi^\star$.
The global algebra, trace, and CP-purity condition are unchanged.

The hull net is generally not local.
For example, $\{0,2\}$ and $\{1\}$ are disjoint, but their hull algebras overlap at site $1$ and need not commute.
Thus this reformulation changes the net as well as the collection of supports on which control is tested.
Requiring control on all bounded sets of the original spin-system net would give a stronger condition.
