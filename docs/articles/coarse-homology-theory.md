# Coarse homology theory

```{warning}
Agent-generated, not reviewed yet
```

A coarse homology theory assigns algebraic invariants to a space in a way which
is insensitive to bounded changes and permits excision along large-scale
decompositions.

Throughout, if $E : \mathrm{BornCoarse} \to C$ is a functor to some (higher) category $C$, and $\mathcal{Y}$ is a big family in a bornological coarse space $X$, we write

$$
E(\mathcal{Y}) := \operatorname*{colim}_{Y \in \mathcal{Y}} E(Y)
$$

Here $Y \subseteq X$ inherits the structure of a bornological coarse space by cutting down entourages and bounded sets to $Y$ and the colimit is taken in the category $C$, observing that $\mathcal{Y}$ forms a directed set.
Here we assume that all filtered colimits exist in $C$.

$\mathcal Y\Cap\mathcal Z$ and $\mathcal Y\Cup\mathcal Z$ denote
the operations on {ref}`big families <article-big-family>`.

## Group-valued theories

```{prf:definition} Group-valued coarse homology theory
:nonumber:
:label: def-group-valued-coarse-homology

A *group-valued coarse homology theory* consists of functors
$E_n:\mathrm{BornCoarse}\to\mathrm{Ab}$, $n\in\mathbb Z$, and natural
Mayer–Vietoris boundary homomorphisms, with the following properties.

1. *Coarse invariance.* Close maps induce equal homomorphisms.
2. *Flasqueness.* $E_n(X)=0$ if $X$ is flasque.
3. *Excision.* For every pair of big families there is a natural exact sequence

   $$
   \cdots\longrightarrow E_n(\mathcal Y\Cap\mathcal Z)
   \xrightarrow{(i_*,-j_*)}E_n(\mathcal Y)\oplus E_n(\mathcal Z)
   \longrightarrow E_n(\mathcal Y\Cup\mathcal Z)
   \xrightarrow{\partial}E_{n-1}(\mathcal Y\Cap\mathcal Z)
   \longrightarrow\cdots,
   $$

   using the standard notation for {ref}`elementwise intersection and union <sec-elementwise-intersection-union>`.

4. *$u$-continuity.* If $X_{\langle E\rangle}$ denotes the bornological coarse space with the same underlying set and bornology as $X$, but the coarse structure generated by $E$, then

   $$
   \operatorname*{colim}_{E\in\mathcal C_X}E_n(X_{\langle E\rangle})\cong E_n(X).
   $$

```

The boundary maps are part of the structure, not just an assertion that some
exact sequence exists. A coarsely excisive cover $X=A\cup B$ gives the usual
sequence involving $A$, $B$, and $A\cap B$. Its excision condition is
$\{A\}\Cap\{B\}=\{A\cap B\}$. An arbitrary cover is instead treated
using the intersection of the generated big families.

## The stable infinity-category formulation

Let $\mathcal D$ be a cocomplete stable $\infty$-category.

```{prf:definition} Coarse homology theory with stable target
:nonumber:
:label: def-stable-coarse-homology

A functor $E:\mathrm{BornCoarse}\to\mathcal D$ is a *coarse homology theory* if
it is coarsely invariant, vanishes on flasque spaces, satisfies excision, and is
$u$-continuous. More precisely:

- the projection $\{0,1\}\otimes X\to X$ induces an equivalence, with
  $\{0,1\}$ carrying the maximal coarse structure and bornology;
- $E(X)\simeq0$ when $X$ is flasque;
- for all big families $\mathcal Y,\mathcal Z$, the canonical square with
  vertices $E(\mathcal Y\Cap\mathcal Z)$, $E(\mathcal Y)$,
  $E(\mathcal Z)$, and $E(\mathcal Y\Cup\mathcal Z)$ is a pushout;
- $\operatorname*{colim}_{U\in\mathcal C_X}E(X_{\langle U\rangle})\to E(X)$
  is an equivalence.

Evaluation on a big family is a colimit in $\mathcal D$.
```

Equivalently, the excision square gives a cofiber sequence

$$
E(\mathcal Y\Cap\mathcal Z)\longrightarrow
E(\mathcal Y)\oplus E(\mathcal Z)\longrightarrow E(\mathcal Y\Cup\mathcal Z).
$$

In a stable category a pushout square is also a pullback square. This keeps track
of the boundary maps and their compatibility. For $\mathcal D=\mathrm{Sp}$,
taking $\pi_n$ gives the group-valued theory above. For chain complexes one
first inverts quasi-isomorphisms and then takes homology. The group-valued
axioms alone do not specify all the coherent data of a spectrum-valued lift.

## Examples and suspension

The basic examples are {ref}`ordinary coarse homology
<article-ordinary-coarse-homology>`, with values in the derived category of
abelian groups, and {ref}`coarse K-theory <article-coarse-k-theory>`, with
values in spectra, more precisely $KU$-module spectra in the complex case.
The {ref}`QCA article <article-quantum-cellular-automaton>` describes a further
example, including its stabilization and quotient conventions.

The decomposition of $X\otimes\mathbb R$ into its positive and negative
half-spaces gives

$$
E(X\otimes\mathbb R)\simeq\Sigma E(X).
$$

Indeed, both half-spaces are flasque, and the intersection of their generated
big families is generated by $X\times\{0\}$. Thus a line direction shifts
the homological degree by one. For example,
$HX_k(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb Z)$ is $\mathbb Z$ for $k=n$ and zero otherwise.

## Coarsening an ordinary homology theory

The input is a topological homology theory together with its locally finite
extension. For a spectrum-valued homology theory $h$, the latter is modeled by

$$
h^{\mathrm{lf}}(Y)
 =\lim_K\operatorname{Cofib}\bigl(h(Y\setminus K)\longrightarrow h(Y)\bigr),
$$

with $K$ ranging over compact subsets. Homotopy limits and cofibers are taken
in the target category. On a space with a specified bornology one uses bounded
subsets and the corresponding bornological version.

For a proper discrete metric space, let $P_R(X)$ be the Rips complex: a finite
subset spans a simplex when its diameter is at most $R$. These are locally
finite simplicial complexes. A standard coarsening construction is

$$
h\mathcal X(X)=\operatorname*{colim}_{R\to\infty}
                   h^{\mathrm{lf}}(P_R(X)).
$$

A coarse map induces simplicial maps after increasing the control parameter.
Close maps induce contiguous maps after a further increase. The scale colimit
therefore forgets small-scale choices.

For arbitrary bornological coarse spaces, a bornological Rips construction
and locally finite homology on topological bornological spaces are used.
The general construction requires the hypotheses on the target and the locally
finite theory given in their coarsification theorem. In particular it is not
obtained by simply applying ordinary, non-locally-finite homology to the set
$X$. Applied to ordinary homology it yields ordinary coarse homology; applied
to stable homotopy it yields a coarse stable homotopy theory. The general
coarsification theorem is proved in
[Bunke–Engel, Homotopy theory with bornological coarse spaces, Chapter 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03657).

## From coarse to ordinary homology via cones

A cone construction needs uniform information in addition to a coarse
structure. Let $X$ be a uniform bornological coarse space. The two-sided cone
$\mathcal O^\infty(X)$ has underlying set
$\mathbb R\times X$ and the product bornology. Its entourages are product
coarse entourages whose $X$-components become uniformly small towards
$+\infty$: for every uniform entourage $V$, there is a $T$ such that

$$
((t,x),(s,y))\in U,\quad \min(t,s)\geq T
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (x,y)\in V.
$$

For a *strong* coarse homology theory $E$, the functor

$$
F(X)=\Sigma^{-1}E(\mathcal O^\infty(X))
$$

is a local homology theory. Here strong means that $E$ also vanishes on weakly
flasque spaces: spaces with a uniformly controlled, escaping self-map as in
flasqueness, whose action on the universal coarse homology object is equivalent
to the identity, even if the map itself is not close to the identity.
This is a stronger requirement on $E$ than the four basic axioms. The local
homology property is established in
[Bunke–Ludewig, Coronas and Callias type operators in coarse geometry, §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01646).

The identity map from the cone to $\mathbb R\otimes X$ gives an index map
$F(X)\to E(X)$ after the suspension identification. Suppose that $E$ is also
*additive*, meaning that it takes free unions of one-point spaces to products
of $E(*)$, and that its target is complete. The comparison theorem in
[Bunke–Ludewig, §2.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01646) identifies $F$ on finite
simplicial complexes, with their compact uniform structures, with the homology
theory having coefficient object $E(*)$.
That ordinary theory extends to CW complexes by colimits; a locally finite
version uses the locally finite extension. These qualifications distinguish
the local cone construction from an unqualified homology theory on all
topological spaces.

For $E=K\mathcal X$, this recovers the familiar passage from local
$K$-homology classes to coarse index classes.
