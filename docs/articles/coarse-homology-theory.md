# Coarse homology theory

A coarse homology theory assigns algebraic invariants to a space in a way which is insensitive to bounded changes and permits excision along large-scale decompositions.

Throughout, if $E : \BornCoarse \to C$ is a functor to some (higher) category $C$, and $\cY$ is a {prf:ref}`big family <def-big-family>` in a bornological coarse space $X$, we write
$$
E(\cY) := \mathop{\colim}_{Y \in \cY} E(Y)
$$
Here $Y \subseteq X$ inherits the structure of a bornological coarse space by cutting down entourages and bounded sets to $Y$ and the colimit is taken in the category $C$, observing that $\cY$ forms a directed set.
Here we assume that all filtered colimits exist in $C$.

$\cY\Cap\cZ$ and $\cY\Cup\cZ$ denote the operations on {prf:ref}`big families <def-big-family>`.

## Group-valued theories

```{prf:definition} Group-valued coarse homology theory
:nonumber:
:label: def-group-valued-coarse-homology

A *group-valued coarse homology theory* consists of functors $E_n:\BornCoarse\to\Ab$, $n\in\Z$, and natural Mayer–Vietoris boundary homomorphisms, with the following properties.

1. *Coarse invariance.*
   Close maps induce equal homomorphisms.
2. *Flasqueness.*
   $E_n(X)=0$ if $X$ is flasque.
3. *Excision.*
   For every pair of {prf:ref}`big families <def-big-family>` there is a natural exact sequence
   $$
   \cdots\longrightarrow E_n(\cY\Cap\cZ)
   \xrightarrow{(i_*,-j_*)}E_n(\cY)\oplus E_n(\cZ)
   \longrightarrow E_n(\cY\Cup\cZ)
   \xrightarrow{\partial}E_{n-1}(\cY\Cap\cZ)
   \longrightarrow\cdots,
   $$
   using the standard notation for {ref}`elementwise intersection and union <sec-elementwise-intersection-union>`.

4. *$u$-continuity.*
   If $X_{\langle E\rangle}$ denotes the bornological coarse space with the same underlying set and bornology as $X$, but the coarse structure generated by $E$, then
   $$
   \mathop{\colim}_{E\in\cC_X}E_n(X_{\langle E\rangle})\cong E_n(X).
   $$
```

The boundary maps are part of the structure, not just an assertion that some exact sequence exists.
A coarsely excisive cover $X=A\cup B$ gives the usual sequence involving $A$, $B$, and $A\cap B$.
Its excision condition is $\{A\}\Cap\{B\}=\{A\cap B\}$.
An arbitrary cover is instead treated using the intersection of the generated {prf:ref}`big families <def-big-family>`.

## The stable infinity-category formulation

Let $\cD$ be a cocomplete stable $\infty$-category.

```{prf:definition} Coarse homology theory with stable target
:nonumber:
:label: def-stable-coarse-homology

A *coarse homology theory* is a functor $E:\BornCoarse\to\cD$ with the following properties:
1. *Coarse invariance.*
   Close maps induce equivalent homomorphisms in $D$.
2. *Flasqueness.*
   If $X$ is flasque, then $E_n(X)\simeq 0$, the zero object in $\cD$.
3. *Excision.*
   For every pair of {prf:ref}`big families <def-big-family>` $\cY, \cZ$, the canonical square with vertices $E(\cY\Cap\cZ)$, $E(\cY)$, $E(\cZ)$, and $E(\cY\Cup\cZ)$ is a pushout.

4. *$u$-continuity.*
   The map $\mathop{\colim}_{F\in\cC_X}E(X_{\langle F\rangle})\to E(X)$ is an equivalence.
```

Equivalently, the excision square gives a cofiber sequence
$$
E(\cY\Cap\cZ)\longrightarrow
E(\cY)\oplus E(\cZ)\longrightarrow E(\cY\Cup\cZ).
$$
In a stable category a pushout square is also a pullback square.
This keeps track of the boundary maps and their compatibility.
For $\cD=\Sp$, the stable $\infty$-category of spectra, taking homotopy groups gives the group-valued theory above.
For $\cD$ the $\infty$-category of chain complexes over a ring, one  takes homology.


## Examples and suspension

The basic examples are {ref}`ordinary coarse homology <article-ordinary-coarse-homology>`, with values in the derived category of abelian groups, and {ref}`coarse K-theory <article-coarse-k-theory>`, with values in spectra, more precisely $KU$-module spectra in the complex case.
The {ref}`QCA article <article-quantum-cellular-automaton>` describes a further example, including its stabilization and quotient conventions.

The decomposition of $X\otimes\R$ into its positive and negative half-spaces gives
$$
E(X\otimes\R)\simeq\Sigma E(X).
$$
Indeed, both half-spaces are flasque, and the intersection of their generated {prf:ref}`big families <def-big-family>` is generated by $X\times\{0\}$.
Thus a line direction shifts the homological degree by one.
