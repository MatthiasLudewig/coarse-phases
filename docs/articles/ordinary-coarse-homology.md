# Ordinary coarse homology

Ordinary coarse homology is the coarse analog of Alexander–Spanier homology of topological spaces.

## Coarse homology groups

Let $(X, \cB, \cC)$ be a bornological coarse space and let $A$ be an abelian group.

```{prf:definition} Coarse chains
:nonumber:
:label: def-coarse-chain

A *coarse chain of degree* $n$ is a function $c : X^{n+1} \to A$ whose support is {prf:ref}`locally finite <def-locally-finite-subset>` and contained in the $E$-{ref}`fattening <def-fattening>` of the multidiagonal $\Delta \subset X^{n+1}$ for some entourage $E$.

We denote the group of degree $n$ chains by $C\cX_n(X, A)$.
```

Here by $X^{n+1}$, we mean the coarse space obtained by taking the $(n+1)$-fold {prf:ref}`tensor product <def-coarse-tensor-product>` of $X$ with itself.
The spaces of chains assemble to a chain complex $C\cX(X, A)$ with the usual Alexander–Spanier boundary map
$$
(\partial c) (x_0,\ldots,x_n)
 =\sum_{j=0}^{n+1} (-1)^j \sum_{y \in X} c(x_0,\ldots,x_{j-1}, y, x_j,\ldots,x_n)
$$
for chains $c$ of positive degree $n+1$ and the zero boundary map in degree zero.
Here the sum is actually a finite sum by the support conditions on $c$.

(def-coarse-homology-groups)=

```{prf:definition} Coarse homology groups
:nonumber:
:label: def-ordinary-coarse-homology

The *coarse homology groups* of $X$ with coefficients in $A$ are the homology groups $H\cX_n(X, A)$ of the chain complex $C\cX(X, A)$.
```

If $\cY$ is a {prf:ref}`big family <def-big-family>` in $X$, then
$$
C\cX_n(\cY, A) = \colim\displaylimits_{Y \in \cY} C\cX_n(Y, A)
$$
may be described as the subgroup of $C\cX_n(X, A)$ consisting of chains that are supported on $Y^{n+1}$ for some member $Y$ of $\cY$.
Because taking homology groups commutes with filtered colimits, the homology groups $H\cX(\cY, A)$ of this complex may be computed as the colimit of the coarse homology groups $H\cX(Y, A)$.

## The coarse homology theory

Throughout, let $A$ be an abelian group.
A map of coarse spaces $f:X\to Y$ induces a chain map $f_* : C\cX(X, A) \to C\cX(Y, A)$ given by
$$
(f_*c)(y_0, \ldots, y_n)
= \sum_{x_0 \in f^{-1}(y_0)} \cdots \sum_{x_n \in f^{-1}(y_n)} c(x_0, \ldots, x_n).
$$
Properness of $f$ makes the sums over fibers finite, and controlledness of $f$ preserves the control condition.
We therefore obtain a functor
$$
C\cX(-;A):\BornCoarse \longrightarrow \mathrm{Ch}(\Ab).
$$
This functor has the following properties ([Bunke–Engel, Homotopy theory with bornological coarse spaces, §6.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03657)):

1. If $f, g: X \to Y$ are close maps, then $f_*$ and $g_*$ are chain homotopic.
2. If $X$ is flasque, then $C\cX(X, A)$ is acyclic (has zero homology).
3. If $\cY$ and $\cZ$ are {prf:ref}`big families <def-big-family>` in $X$, there is a long exact sequence
   $$
   \cdots\longrightarrow H\cX_n(\cY\Cap\cZ)
   \xrightarrow{(i_*,-j_*)}H\cX_n(\cY)\oplus H\cX_n(\cZ)
   \longrightarrow H\cX_n(\cY\Cup\cZ)
   \xrightarrow{\partial}H\cX_{n-1}(\cY\Cap\cZ)
   \longrightarrow\cdots,
   $$
4. For an entourage $E$ of $X$, denoting by $X_{\langle E\rangle}$ the space with coarse structure generated by $E$, we have
   $$
   C\cX(X, A) = \colim\displaylimits_{E \in \cC} C\cX(X_{\langle E \rangle}, A).
   $$

Consequently, the composition
$$
\BornCoarse \xrightarrow{~C\cX(-, A)~} \mathrm{Ch}(\Ab) \longrightarrow D(\Z)
$$
is a coarse homology theory, where $D(\Z)$ is the *derived category of* $\Z$, i.e., the $\infty$-category obtained from the category of chain complexes of abelian groups by inverting quasi-isomorphisms.

## Coeffients

For $X = \mathrm{pt}$ a point, the only non-trivial coarse homology group is $H \cX_0(\mathrm{pt}, A) = A$.
Consequently, the coarse homology groups of Euclidean spaces are
$$
H\cX_k(\Z^n, A) = H\cX_k(\R^n, A) =
\begin{cases}
A & k = n
\\
0 & k \neq n.
\end{cases}
$$
