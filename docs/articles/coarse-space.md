# Coarse space

Coarse geometry concerns the geometry of a space at large scales. 
Any metric space yields a coarse space by only remembering the large scale geometry.
However, metric spaces also carry the information of a topology, even a uniform structure on the underlying set.
This information on small scale structure is discarded when passing to the corresponding coarse space.
Moreover, not every coarse structure on a set is induced by a metric space.


## Definitions


```{prf:definition} Coarse structure
:nonumber:
:label: def-coarse-structure

A *coarse structure* on a set $X$ is a collection $\mathcal C$ of subsets of $X\times X$ containing the diagonal $\Delta_X = \{(x, x) \mid x \in X\}$, which is closed under subsets, finite unions, inverses, and compositions. 
Its members are called *entourages*.
```

Here for subsets $E, F\subseteq X\times X$, the inverse and composition is defined by

$$
E^{-1}=\{(y,x):(x,y)\in E\},\qquad
E\circ F=\{(x,z):\exists y,\ (x,y)\in E,\ (y,z)\in F\}.
$$

```{prf:definition} Bornological space
:nonumber:
:label: def-bornology

A *bornology* on a set $X$ is a collection $\mathcal B$ of subsets of $X$, closed under subsets and finite unions, which contains every singleton. 
Its members are called *bounded sets*. 
A *bornological space* is a set equipped with a bornology.
```

For an entourage $E$ and a subset $Y\subseteq X$, the *$E$-fattening* of $Y$ is

(def-fattening)=

```{math}
Y_E:=\{x\in X:\exists y\in Y,\ (x,y)\in E\}.
```

This concept is used to formulate the following compatibility condition between coarse structure and bornology, requiring that a uniformly controlled thickening of a bounded set is again bounded.

```{prf:definition} Bornological coarse space
:nonumber:
:label: def-bornological-coarse-space

A *bornological coarse space* is a triple $(X,\mathcal C,\mathcal B)$ such that
$B_E\in\mathcal B$ whenever $E\in\mathcal C$ and $B\in\mathcal B$.
```

## The canonical bornology

For a general coarse structure $\mathcal{C}$ on a set $X$, the *canonical bornology* is the bornology consisting of finite unions of subsets $B \subseteq X$ such that $B \times B$ is an entourage.

If $X$ is *coarsely connected*, meaning that $\bigcup\mathcal C=X\times X$,
finite unions of coarsely bounded sets are again coarsely bounded. 
In that case the canonical bornology is simply $\{B:B\times B\in\mathcal C\}$. 
Without coarse connectedness the finite-union qualification is necessary. 

The maximal bornology $\mathcal P(X)$ is compatible with every coarse structure.
Thus the bornology is genuinely additional data, even when a natural choice is
available.


In applications, we are mostly interested in the canonical bornology. 
However, it is sometimes convenient to allow this additional flexibility of choosing the bornology.

## Examples

A metric $d$ defines the *metric coarse structure*

$$
\mathcal C_d=\{E\subseteq X\times X:\exists R<\infty,\
E\subseteq E_R\},\qquad E_R=\{(x,y):d(x,y)\leq R\}.
$$

Together with the finite-diameter subsets this gives the usual bornological
coarse structure on a metric space. 

The *coarse structure generated by* a collection $\mathcal{E}$ of subsets of $X \times X$ is the smallest coarse structure containing that collection.
Explicitly, it is the intersection of all coarse structures containing $\mathcal{E}$.

## Coarse maps and equivalences

```{prf:definition} Coarse map
:nonumber:
:label: def-coarse-map

A map $f:X\to Y$ is *controlled* if $(f\times f)(E)$ is an entourage of $Y$ for
every entourage $E$ of $X$. 
It is *proper* if $f^{-1}(B)$ is bounded for every bounded subset $B$ of $Y$. 
A *coarse map* is a map having both properties.
```

For metric structures, controlledness says that for every $R$ there is an $S$
such that $d_X(x,x')\leq R$ implies $d_Y(f(x),f(x'))\leq S$.
Continuity is not part of this definition. 
In an analytic construction which uses Borel functional calculus, measurability will be imposed separately.

```{prf:definition} Closeness and coarse equivalence
:nonumber:
:label: def-coarse-equivalence

Maps $f,g:X\to Y$ are *close* if $\{(f(x),g(x)):x\in X\}$ is an entourage of $Y$. 
A coarse map $f$ is a *coarse equivalence* if it has a coarse inverse up to closeness: there is a coarse map $g:Y\to X$ with $gf$ and $fg$ close to the
respective identity maps.
```

The inclusion $\mathbb Z^n\hookrightarrow\mathbb R^n$ is a coarse equivalence; a choice of nearest lattice point gives a coarse inverse.

## The category of bornological coarse spaces

We write $\mathrm{BornCoarse}$ for the category with bornological coarse spaces as objects and coarse maps as morphisms. 
Compositions of controlled maps are controlled, and compositions of proper maps are proper. 
Subspace inclusions are coarse. 
The tensor product below is functorial on this category.

The morphisms remember actual maps, not just their closeness classes.
{ref}`Coarse homology theories <article-coarse-homology-theory>` send close
morphisms to the same homomorphism, or to homotopic morphisms in a stable target.

## Tensor product

```{prf:definition} Tensor product of coarse spaces
:nonumber:
:label: def-coarse-tensor-product

The *tensor product* $X\otimes Y$ has underlying set $X\times Y$. 
Its bornology consists of subsets of $B\times B'$ with $B \subseteq X$ and $B' \subseteq Y$ bounded. 
Its coarse structure is generated by the subsets

$$
\{((x,y),(x',y')):(x,x')\in E,\ (y,y')\in E'\}
$$

for entourages $E$ of $X$ and $E'$ of $Y$.

```

This makes the category of bornological coarse space symmetric monoidal, with the one-point space as tensor unit. 
For metric spaces it agrees with the structures induced by the maximum
product metric. 
In particular, $\mathbb R^m\otimes\mathbb R^n\cong \mathbb R^{m+n}$.

This tensor product is **not the categorical product** in bornological coarse spaces. 
The latter has the same coarse structure but the bornology generated by the cylinders $B\times Y$ and $X\times B'$. 
This larger bornology makes both projections proper. 
A projection $X\otimes Y\to X$ need not be proper when $Y$ is unbounded.
