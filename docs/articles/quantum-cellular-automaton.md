# Quantum cellular automaton

```{warning}
Agent-generated, not reviewed yet
```

A quantum cellular automaton is an automorphism of a spin system which moves
local observables only a uniformly controlled distance. Translation invariance
is not required. Classification requires specifying both the allowed
stabilizations and which local operations are declared trivial.

## Controlled automorphisms of local nets

```{prf:definition} Quantum cellular automaton
:label: def-qca

Let $\mathcal A$ be a local matrix net on $X$. A quantum cellular automaton
(QCA) is a $*$-automorphism $\alpha$ of $\mathcal A_X$ such that $\alpha$ and
$\alpha^{-1}$ are controlled: each sends $\mathcal A_B$ into
$\mathcal A_{E[B]}$ for one entourage, independent of the bounded set $B$.
```

For a matrix net, controlledness of the inverse also follows from that of the
automorphism, by commuting with observables in complementary regions. On a
metric space this is the usual finite-range condition. A shift of identical
matrix factors on $\mathbb Z$ is an example.

A layer of local gates acts independently on a locally finite collection of
uniformly bounded blocks. A finite-depth quantum circuit is a finite
composition of such layers. For arbitrary coarse spaces the supplied
manuscript uses the following slightly more flexible notion.

A **coarsely local automorphism** preserves a decomposition of the net into
commuting tensor factors supported on a locally finite uniformly bounded
family $(B_i)$, and acts separately on those factors. Let
$\mathrm{LAut}(\mathcal A)$ be the normal subgroup generated by these
automorphisms. On the bounded-geometry lattice models of interest this gives
the usual circuit subgroup. The resulting stable group is
$$
\mathrm{QCA}(X)=\operatorname*{colim}_{\mathcal A}
 \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(\mathcal A)/\mathrm{LAut}(\mathcal A),
$$
where stabilization sends $\alpha$ to $\alpha\otimes\mathrm{id}$ on an
additional local matrix net. Identifications of pointwise tensor factors use
propagation-zero isomorphisms. All locally finite assignments of finite matrix
dimensions are allowed. Stacking supplies the abelian group operation.

## The associated coarse homology theory

The construction in the author's {ref}`QCA manuscript <bib-qca-notes>` uses
the symmetric monoidal groupoid $\mathrm{Az}(X)$ of Azumaya nets and
controlled isomorphisms; see {ref}`Controlled objects <article-controlled-objects>`.
Here $K$ denotes the connective spectrum obtained by group-completing a
symmetric monoidal groupoid, not the topological $K$-theory of a Roe algebra.

The proposed spectrum is
$$
Q(X)=\operatorname*{colim}_{m\geq0}
       \Omega^{m+1}K\bigl(\mathrm{Az}(X\otimes\mathbb R^m)\bigr).
$$
The structure maps come from the Mayer–Vietoris boundary for the decomposition
of the additional line into two half-lines. The manuscript proves the
identification
$$
\pi_0Q(X)\cong\mathrm{QCA}(X)
$$
and states and proves that $Q$ satisfies the coarse homology axioms.
The extra loop in the definition places automorphisms, represented in $K_1$,
in degree zero. The iterated delooping is needed because connective
$K$-theory alone does not supply excision in all degrees.

This paragraph records the construction and claims of the supplied manuscript;
the present draft does not constitute an independent verification of its
spectrum-level proof. In particular, its groupoid and stabilization conventions
are part of the definition and cannot be replaced silently by a fixed spin
system or by a norm-topologized automorphism group.

## The GNVW index

On $\mathbb Z$, group cells into finite blocks so that a QCA has nearest-neighbour
range. Write the cell algebra as $\mathcal A_j=M_{d_j}(\mathbb C)$.
For the image of $\mathcal A_{2j}\otimes\mathcal A_{2j+1}$, let
$\mathcal R_{2j}$ and $\mathcal R_{2j+1}$ be its minimal support algebras in
the blocks $(2j-1,2j)$ and $(2j+1,2j+2)$. A support algebra is the smallest
subalgebra of the indicated tensor factor containing all matrix coefficients
of the image with respect to the other factor.

The support-algebra theorem of Gross–Nesme–Vogts–Werner shows that
$\mathcal R_k=M_{r_k}(\mathbb C)$ and that
$$
\operatorname{ind}(\alpha)
 =\frac{r_{2j}}{d_{2j}}
 =\frac{d_{2j+1}}{r_{2j+1}}\in\mathbb Q_{>0}
$$
is independent of $j$ and regrouping. This is their orientation convention:
the shift carrying the algebra at $j$ to that at $j-1$ has index $d$ when
all cell dimensions equal $d$. Reversing the direction inverts the index.

The index is multiplicative under both composition and stacking. Index one
characterizes circuit-trivial QCA in the one-dimensional setting of the
theorem. Allowing arbitrary ancillary matrix factors gives
$$
\mathrm{QCA}(\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Q_{>0},
$$
with multiplication on the right. In the manuscript's homological formulation,
this is the boundary isomorphism with
$K_0(\mathrm{Az}(*))$: the monoid of matrix sizes is the positive integers
under multiplication, whose group completion is $\mathbb Q_{>0}$.

## Vanishing on the plane

Haah proves that the one-dimensional Brauer group of invertible subalgebras
is zero. In the manuscript's identification with Azumaya nets, this says that
one-dimensional Azumaya data become local matrix data after stabilization.
Every local matrix net on the line splits into its two half-line restrictions;
the half-line swindles make their classes zero in the group completion.
Together with the manuscript's boundary comparison, this gives
$$
\mathrm{QCA}(\mathbb R^2)\cong\mathrm{QCA}(\mathbb Z^2)
 \cong K_0(\mathrm{Az}(\mathbb Z))=0.
$$
This is vanishing of the **stable coarse quotient** defined above. Haah's
statement about lattice QCA is formulated using blending and shifts; it does
not assert that every automorphism on a fixed lattice is the identity or a
circuit on that same fixed spin system. The comparison of conventions is part
of the passage to the coarse group. No proof of Haah's theorem is supplied
here. See [Invertible subalgebras, Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.02086v4).

## Sources

The coarse construction and comparison with Azumaya nets follow the author's
{ref}`QCA manuscript <bib-qca-notes>`, §§2–3. The index is
{ref}`GNVW <bib-gnvw>`, §7, especially Theorem 9.
