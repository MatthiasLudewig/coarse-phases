# Quantum cellular automaton

```{warning}
Agent-generated, not reviewed yet
```

A quantum cellular automaton is an automorphism of a spin system which moves local observables only a uniformly controlled distance.
Translation invariance is not required.
Classification requires specifying both the allowed stabilizations and which local operations are declared trivial.

## Controlled automorphisms of local nets

```{prf:definition} Quantum cellular automaton
:nonumber:
:label: def-qca

Let $\mathcal A$ be a local matrix net on $X$.
A *quantum cellular automaton* (QCA) is a $*$-automorphism $\alpha$ of $\mathcal A_X$ such that $\alpha$ and $\alpha^{-1}$ are controlled: each sends $\mathcal A_B$ into $\mathcal A_{B_E}$ for one entourage, independent of the bounded set $B$.
```

For a matrix net, controlledness of the inverse also follows from that of the automorphism, by commuting with observables in complementary regions.
On a metric space this is the usual finite-range condition.
A shift of identical matrix factors on $\mathbb Z$ is an example.

A layer of local gates acts independently on a locally finite collection of uniformly bounded blocks.
A *finite-depth quantum circuit* is a finite composition of such layers.
On arbitrary coarse spaces it is useful to allow the following more flexible notion.

A *coarsely local automorphism* preserves a decomposition of the net into commuting tensor factors supported on a locally finite uniformly bounded family $(B_i)$, and acts separately on those factors.
Let $\mathrm{LAut}(\mathcal A)$ be the normal subgroup generated by these automorphisms.
For nets with uniformly locally finite support this is the usual circuit subgroup; see [Ludewig, Proposition 2.43](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10501).
The resulting stable group is
$$
\mathrm{QCA}(X)=\operatorname*{colim}_{\mathcal A}
 \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(\mathcal A)/\mathrm{LAut}(\mathcal A),
$$
where stabilization sends $\alpha$ to $\alpha\otimes\mathrm{id}$ on an additional local matrix net.
Identifications of pointwise tensor factors use propagation-zero isomorphisms.
All locally finite assignments of finite matrix dimensions are allowed.
Stacking supplies the abelian group operation.

## The associated coarse homology theory

Let $\mathrm{Az}(X)$ be the symmetric monoidal groupoid of Azumaya nets and controlled isomorphisms; see {ref}`Controlled objects <article-controlled-objects>`.
Here $K$ denotes the connective spectrum obtained by group-completing a symmetric monoidal groupoid, not the topological $K$-theory of a Roe algebra.

Define the spectrum
$$
Q(X)=\operatorname*{colim}_{m\geq0}
       \Omega^{m+1}K\bigl(\mathrm{Az}(X\otimes\mathbb R^m)\bigr).
$$
The structure maps come from the Mayer–Vietoris boundary for the decomposition of the additional line into two half-lines.
There is a natural identification
$$
\pi_0Q(X)\cong\mathrm{QCA}(X)
$$
and $Q$ satisfies the coarse homology axioms.
These are [Ludewig's Theorems 3.2 and 3.13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10501).
The extra loop in the definition places automorphisms, represented in $K_1$, in degree zero.
The iterated delooping is needed because connective $K$-theory alone does not supply excision in all degrees.

The groupoid and stabilization conventions are part of this construction.
Fixing a single spin system or equipping its automorphism group with a norm topology would define a different object.

## The GNVW index

On $\mathbb Z$, group cells into finite blocks so that a QCA has nearest-neighbour range.
Write the cell algebra as $\mathcal A_j=M_{d_j}(\mathbb C)$.
For the image of $\mathcal A_{2j}\otimes\mathcal A_{2j+1}$, let $\mathcal R_{2j}$ and $\mathcal R_{2j+1}$ be its minimal support algebras in the blocks $(2j-1,2j)$ and $(2j+1,2j+2)$.
A *support algebra* is the smallest subalgebra of the indicated tensor factor containing all matrix coefficients of the image with respect to the other factor.

The support-algebra theorem of [Gross–Nesme–Vogts–Werner, §7, Theorem 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.3675) shows that $\mathcal R_k=M_{r_k}(\mathbb C)$ and that
$$
\operatorname{ind}(\alpha)
 =\frac{r_{2j}}{d_{2j}}
 =\frac{d_{2j+1}}{r_{2j+1}}\in\mathbb Q_{>0}
$$
is independent of $j$ and regrouping.
This is their orientation convention: the shift carrying the algebra at $j$ to that at $j-1$ has index $d$ when all cell dimensions equal $d$.
Reversing the direction inverts the index.

The index is multiplicative under both composition and stacking.
Index one characterizes circuit-trivial QCA in the one-dimensional setting of the theorem.
Allowing arbitrary ancillary matrix factors gives
$$
\mathrm{QCA}(\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Q_{>0},
$$
with multiplication on the right.
In the homological formulation, this is the [boundary isomorphism of Ludewig, Theorem 3.15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10501), with $K_0(\mathrm{Az}(*))$: the monoid of matrix sizes is the positive integers under multiplication, whose group completion is $\mathbb Q_{>0}$.

## Vanishing on the plane

[Haah, Theorem B.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02086v4), proves that the one-dimensional Brauer group of invertible subalgebras is zero.
In terms of Azumaya nets, this says that one-dimensional Azumaya data become local matrix data after stabilization.
Every local matrix net on the line splits into its two half-line restrictions; the half-line swindles make their classes zero in the group completion.
Together with the boundary isomorphism above, this gives
$$
\mathrm{QCA}(\mathbb R^2)\cong\mathrm{QCA}(\mathbb Z^2)
 \cong K_0(\mathrm{Az}(\mathbb Z))=0.
$$
This is vanishing of the **stable coarse quotient** defined above.
Haah's statement about lattice QCA is formulated using blending and shifts; it does not assert that every automorphism on a fixed lattice is the identity or a circuit on that same fixed spin system.
The comparison of conventions is part of the passage to the coarse group.
No proof of Haah's theorem is supplied here.

These statements concern bosonic complex matrix algebras.
Fermionic and symmetry-constrained variants require separate definitions.
